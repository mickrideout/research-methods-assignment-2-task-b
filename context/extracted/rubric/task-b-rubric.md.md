## Task B: Working with Hypotheses (10 marks)

| Criterion | N | P | C | D | HD | Feedback |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Marks** | **0** | **5** | **6.25** | **7.5** | **10** | |
| **Hypothesis and null hypothesis are relevant to the given topic (2 marks)** | Neither a valid hypothesis nor a valid null hypothesis is provided. (0 marks) | A hypothesis and/or null hypothesis is provided, but is incorrectly structured, not clearly testable, or not relevant to the topic. The null hypothesis may not logically correspond to the alternative hypothesis. (1 mark) | A relevant hypothesis and null hypothesis are provided and broadly related to each other and the topic. However, variables are implicit, the predicted relationship (or absence of relationship) is unclear, and testability is only partially evident. (1.25 marks) | The hypothesis and null hypothesis are clearly stated, include named variables, and are logically paired. The predicted relationship and absence of relationship are evident, although wording, precision, or testability could be further refined. (1.5 marks) | The hypothesis and null hypothesis are correctly formulated, clearly linked, and fully testable. Variables are explicitly named, the predicted relationship is clearly stated, and the null hypothesis explicitly states the absence of that relationship as a logical counterpart. (2 marks) | |
| **Variables (2 marks)** | No variables identified. (0 marks) | Independent and dependent variables are identified, but are not appropriate to the hypothesis or their roles are confused. No confounding variables identified. (1 mark) | Independent and dependent variables are correctly identified and appropriate to the hypothesis. Fewer than three confounding variables are provided, or the confounding variables listed are not relevant to this topic. (1.25 marks) | Independent, dependent, and at least three confounding variables are identified and appropriate to the hypothesis. Relevance of some confounding variables to the topic is not clear. (1.5 marks) | Independent, dependent, and at least three confounding variables are correctly identified. All are clearly appropriate to the hypothesis and the confounding variables are relevant to the topic. (2 marks) | |
| **Data Collection (2 marks)** | Details on data collection not provided. (0 marks) | A data collection method is described, but it is not feasible, OR not related to the hypothesis, OR makes no reference to the $\$1000$ budget and the time allocated ($100\text{ hours}$). (1 mark) | A feasible method is described and linked to the hypothesis. The plan is general and lacks practical detail on sampling, recruitment, processes, or equipment. No reference to the $\$1000$ budget and the time allocation ($100\text{ hours}$). (1.25 marks) | A feasible and practically detailed plan is described with clear relevance to the hypothesis. Sampling, recruitment, processes, and equipment are addressed, but the $\$1000$ budget and the $100\text{ hour}$ time allocation are not clearly integrated into the plan. (1.5 marks) | A feasible and clearly described data collection plan that is closely related to the hypothesis. Sampling strategy, recruitment approach, location, equipment, and processes are all outlined within a realistic $\$1000$ budget and the $100\text{ hour}$ time allocation. (2 marks) | |
| **Statistical Analysis (2 marks)** | No statistical tests proposed. (0 marks) | Tests are named, but are not appropriate for the variable types or hypothesis. Data assumptions are not addressed and no reasoning is given for why the tests are suitable. (1 mark) | Appropriate tests are named and are relevant to the hypothesis. Data assumptions are not addressed and no explanation is provided for why the tests are appropriate. (1.25 marks) | Appropriate tests are named and relevant to the hypothesis. Some data assumptions are identified, but the explanation of why the tests suit this topic is not fully developed. (1.5 marks) | Appropriate tests are named, relevant to the hypothesis, and data assumptions are clearly identified. A plausible explanation is provided for why each test is appropriate for the variable types and research design, and how results would be interpreted. (2 marks) | |
| **Study Limitations and Alternative Narrative (2 marks)** | No limitations or alternative narrative are provided. (0 marks) | Limitations are absent, generic, or not related to the topic and hypothesis. An alternative narrative is absent, merely restates the original hypothesis, or is not supported by the data. There is little or no consideration of the impact on findings, future investigations, or how different data selections, methods, or processes may lead to alternative interpretations. (1 mark) | Limitations relevant to the topic and hypothesis are identified, and an alternative narrative distinct from the original hypothesis is provided. However, the impact of the limitations on findings is unclear, future investigations are not clearly linked to the limitations, and references to alternative data selections, methods, or processes are limited or unclear. (1.25 marks) | Multiple limitations related to the topic and hypothesis are identified, with some discussion of their impact on findings. A distinct alternative narrative is provided and supported by references to changes in data selection, methods, or processes. Connections between limitations, future investigations, and alternative interpretations are evident, but not fully developed. (1.5 marks) | Multiple well-explained limitations related to the topic and hypothesis are identified, demonstrating critical reflection. Their impact on findings and conclusions is clearly articulated, and proposed future investigations directly address the limitations identified. A realistic and distinct alternative narrative is provided, supported by specific changes to data selection, methods, or processes, with clear justification for how these changes could produce a different interpretation of the findings. (2 marks) | |
