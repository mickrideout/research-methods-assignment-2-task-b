### C5. Study Limitations and Alternative Narrative: level HD, 2/2 marks

- **Evidence:** "A brief, researcher-made feed lacks the personalisation and repeated exposure of real platforms, so any effect may not transfer to everyday use; a multi-day field study with a browser-extension feed could test this." - **Why this level:** There are three design-specific limitations: ecological validity of the stimulus, self-selection in the sampling frame, and an immediate single-item measure. Each states its effect on the conclusions ("may not transfer", "limits generalisation", "overstate lasting change"). Each future investigation answers its own limitation, which satisfies R45, and the sampling limitation covers R53 and R54. Heading 7 gives a distinct conclusion and a specific hypothetical result ("equal near-floor group means"). It names three concrete manipulations: selection bias through the sample, a change of method in measurement timing, and cherry-picking through exclusion based on the manipulation check. Each comes with a mechanism (a floor effect, the effect fading, removing affected participants). It is not just a null outcome or a repeated limitation. All HD observables are met.
- **Missing for the next level up:** n/a. Moderation risk: heading 6 recommends a delayed follow-up as a legitimate test of persistence, but heading 7 treats "measuring likelihood a week later" as a manipulation. A sceptical marker may ask why a legitimate design choice counts as manipulation. The manipulation is really that the immediate rating is dropped and only the delayed one is reported, but the text does not say so. The link from equal means to "reflects finances" also depends on the low-income-only sample and is left implicit.
- **Fixes:** 1. Heading 7 (49/50 words): replace "measuring likelihood a week later, so effects fade" with "reporting only a week-later rating, so effects fade". This is word-neutral (49/50); cutting "then" from "Equal near-floor group means would then 'show'" brings it to 48.
