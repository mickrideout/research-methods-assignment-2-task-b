### C4. Statistical Analysis: level HD, 2/2 marks

- **Evidence:** "Welch's independent-samples t-test will compare mean ownership likelihood between groups. It suits a between-subjects design with two independent groups and a continuous 0–100 outcome and, unlike Student's t-test, does not assume equal variances, which pessimistic framing may disturb by polarising responses." Then: "if floor or ceiling clustering makes scores clearly non-normal, a Mann–Whitney U test will replace it." And: "H0 is rejected if p < .05; H1 is supported only if the pessimistic mean is lower." Assumptions are specific: independence, interval level and within-group normality. One assumption is tied to a fallback test. Interpretation is explicit: a two-sided α = .05 decision rule, a directional check, mean difference, 95% CI and Cohen's d. I checked the power claim ("d = 0.5, 64 per group gives 80% power") independently: 0.80 for a two-sided t-test, df = 126. Every HD observable in rubric.yaml C4 is met.
- **Missing for the next level up:** n/a (top level). Two minor points:

  - The covariates measured in heading 3 are not used (for example, a baseline balance check).
- **Fixes:** 1. Heading 5 is at 146/150 words. If you want covariate use, cut "so an opposite effect is not missed" (7 words) and add "Baseline balance on the heading 3 covariates will be checked" (~9 words; trim elsewhere by 2 words). This is optional and protective, not mark-recovering.

### C5. Study Limitations and Alternative Narrative: level D, 1.5/2 marks

- **Evidence:**
  - Heading 6, limitations, impacts and fixes:
    - "A brief, researcher-made feed lacks the personalisation and repeated exposure of real platforms, so any effect may not transfer to everyday use; a multi-day field study with a browser-extension feed could test this."

  - Heading 7, alternative narrative: "\"Housing pessimism reflects finances, not feeds\" could be manufactured by recruiting only low-income Sydney and Melbourne renters, whose ratings sit near the floor, measuring likelihood a week after exposure, and reporting only the less powerful Mann–Whitney test."
- **Why this level:** Heading 6 is close to HD on its own. It gives three design-specific limitations (artificial stimulus or ecological validity, self-selection, single-item immediate measure), each with a stated impact, including a bias direction for the third ("overstate lasting change"), and each paired with a future investigation. Heading 7 is distinct, realistic and names concrete changes to the participant group, measurement timing and reporting. It goes beyond restating a null outcome, so R44 passes. Two HD observables are only partly met, so under the marking guide's "when in doubt, award the lower level" rule, D is awarded:
  1. *"clear justification for how these changes could produce a different interpretation"*: the reporting manipulation is internally inconsistent with heading 5. Heading 5 already says that floor clustering triggers a switch to Mann–Whitney U. In the manipulated low-income-renter sample whose "ratings sit near the floor", reporting Mann–Whitney is therefore the pre-specified analysis, not a manipulation. The claim that it is "less powerful" is also doubtful for floor-skewed data, where rank tests are often at least as powerful as t-tests. The one-week delay is listed without its mechanism (decay of a short-lived framing effect).
  2. *"proposed future investigations directly address the limitations identified"*: the second fix is only a partial match. The bias named is over-representation of *digitally engaged* young people, but quota sampling by *state and income* rebalances demographics, not digital engagement. It also leaves the sample non-probability.
- **Missing for the next level up (HD):** A heading 7 manipulation that is genuinely a departure from the stated plan, with each change's mechanism made explicit. A second future investigation that targets digital-engagement self-selection.
- **Fixes:**
  1. Heading 7 (46/50 words): replace "and reporting only the less powerful Mann–Whitney test" with a manipulation that departs from the plan. One option is "and excluding pessimistic-condition participants who passed the manipulation check" (selective exclusion or cherry-picking). Add "so any effect fades" after "a week after exposure". To stay under 50 words, shorten "low-income Sydney and Melbourne renters" to "low-income capital-city renters" and drop "resulting". This targets C5 D → HD.
  2. Heading 6 (96/100 words): change "quota sampling by state and income" to a fix that reaches less digitally engaged people. Examples are adding offline recruitment (TAFE campuses, workplaces, community centres) or setting quotas on daily social-media use. Keep the word count roughly neutral. This targets C5 D → HD, together with fix 1.
