## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30 will report higher immediate state housing anxiety after viewing an engagement-ranked feed that clusters negative posts than after viewing the same posts in random order.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among eligible participants, mean immediate state housing anxiety will not differ after engagement-ranked and randomly ordered presentations of the same housing posts.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Within-person independent variable:** feed order, engagement-ranked or random. **Dependent variable:** post-feed six-item state housing-anxiety mean. Counterbalancing addresses order; controlled or measured covariates include baseline anxiety, session order, prior exposure, income, and housing costs.

## 4. Data Collection

A counterbalanced online crossover experiment will recruit 60 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home. University and renter-group notices recruit participants. Each views two eight-minute feeds containing identical posts, separated by a neutral five-minute puzzle; condition order is randomised. Anxiety is measured at baseline with GAD-7 (Spitzer et al., 2006) and after each feed, with carryover checks. Ranked presentation is informed by feed-exposure research (Bakshy et al., 2015). Participants receive AUD 12, costing AUD 720; total cost stays near AUD 800 and workload 85 hours. Consent, debriefing, anonymity, support links, and withdrawal apply.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A paired-samples t-test will compare each participant’s anxiety score after ranked and random feeds, fitting a continuous outcome measured twice on the same individuals. Pairing removes stable between-person differences. The distribution of paired differences, influential values, and independence between participants will be checked; Wilcoxon signed-rank is the fallback for severe non-normality. A prespecified order-stratified sensitivity analysis will examine carryover, and a two-way repeated-measures model will test condition-by-order if carryover appears. Baseline anxiety supports interpretation but is not subtracted from both post-scores because it is shared. At α = .05, the paired mean difference, 95% confidence interval, and Cohen’s dz will be reported. Causal inference is restricted to immediate effects of this simulated ordering under the study conditions.

## 6. Limitations

The washout may not remove emotional carryover, and participants may infer the study purpose after the first feed. A researcher-defined engagement ranking cannot reproduce personal algorithms. Repeated state questions may sensitise responses, while the new housing-anxiety items need validation. The modest convenience sample limits subgroup inference and real-world generalisability. A future parallel-group field study could avoid carryover, expose participants over several days, use validated state measures, and compare multiple transparent ranking rules.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Ignoring condition order, excluding only participants whose second score falls, or calling clustered negative posts equivalent to all algorithmic systems could inflate a harm narrative. Carryover checks and bounded interpretation must be reported regardless of significance.
