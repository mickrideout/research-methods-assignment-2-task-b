## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30 randomly assigned to an engagement-ranked housing feed will report lower immediate homeownership-planning self-efficacy than participants assigned the same posts in chronological order.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Immediate homeownership-planning self-efficacy will not differ between eligible participants randomly assigned to engagement-ranked and chronological versions of the same housing posts.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** randomised feed order, engagement-ranked or chronological. **Dependent variable:** post-exposure mean planning self-efficacy score. Randomisation limits confounding; potential precision covariates are baseline self-efficacy, income, deposit savings, and prior housing-content use.

## 4. Data Collection

An online randomised experiment will recruit 100 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home. University notices and AUD 180 advertising recruit participants. After baseline measures, software presents identical researcher-created posts in one of two orders; ranked feeds can alter exposure opportunities (Bakshy et al., 2015). Participants then complete six pilot-tested self-efficacy items, informed by housing-aspiration research (Parkinson et al., 2019), and an order-recall check. Ten AUD 45 vouchers plus advertising cost about AUD 630; workload is 75 hours. Consent, anonymous IDs, neutral debriefing, secure storage, and no scraping, handles, or private platform data are required.

## 5. Statistical Tests

An independent-samples t-test will compare mean post-exposure self-efficacy across the two randomised groups, matching a between-participants binary manipulation to an approximately continuous outcome. Randomisation permits a causal claim about immediate effects of the simulated ordering. Independence, residual normality, equal variances, and influential values will be checked; Welch’s t-test addresses unequal variances and Mann–Whitney U severe non-normality. A prespecified ANCOVA adjusting for baseline self-efficacy will provide a more precise sensitivity estimate, with income, savings, and prior use added only to examine imbalance. At α = .05, the adjusted or raw mean difference, 95% confidence interval, and Hedges’ g will be reported. Inference will not extend to proprietary real-world algorithms or enduring beliefs.

## 6. Limitations

Engagement ranking is a simplified analogue, not a platform’s opaque personalised system. The same posts in different orders isolate ordering but omit network endorsements and accumulated personalisation. A brief exposure may generate demand effects and only short-lived self-efficacy changes; pilot-tested items lack established external validity. Convenience recruitment restricts generalisability. A future field experiment could randomise a browser-based feed over several days, preregister exposure metrics, validate the outcome scale, and sample beyond university networks while preserving participant control over data.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Excluding participants who noticed the ordering only from one condition, redefining the manipulation check after analysis, or generalising a brief simulated-feed effect to all algorithms could produce a sensational narrative. Prespecified exclusions and bounded claims prevent this.
