## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Within Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, mean perceived housing affordability will be lower on days with above-personal-median social-media housing exposure than on days with below-personal-median exposure.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Within Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, mean perceived housing affordability will not differ between days above and below each participant’s median housing-content exposure.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Within-person predictor:** daily exposure category, above or below personal median minutes. **Outcome:** nightly 1–7 affordability rating. **Time-varying confounders:** daily housing news, financial stress, paid work hours, and mood; stable factors are controlled by pairing.

## 4. Data Collection

A 14-day diary will recruit 45 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home and use social media daily. University and renter-group notices recruit participants. A free survey tool sends nightly links recording housing-content minutes, affordability, news, financial stress, work, and mood. ABS and AHURI sources frame affordability (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2022; Parkinson et al., 2019). Participants completing at least 11 diaries receive AUD 15, costing AUD 675; software costs are nil and workload about 90 hours. Consent, coded IDs, separate payment contacts, reminders, and no screenshots or private posts protect privacy.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A paired-samples t-test will compare, for each participant, their mean affordability rating across above-median and below-median exposure days. Pairing is appropriate because both condition means come from the same individuals and the outcome is treated as approximately interval. The analysis requires participants to contribute at least two days per category. Independence between participants, approximate normality of paired differences, and influential differences will be checked; a Wilcoxon signed-rank test will replace the t-test if differences are severely non-normal. Time-varying confounders will be compared across day categories and discussed rather than overfitting this small sample. At α = .05, the paired mean difference, 95% confidence interval, and paired-samples Cohen’s dz will be reported. Natural exposure remains observational, so temporal co-occurrence is not causation.

## 6. Limitations

Self-reported exposure minutes and single-item nightly affordability are noisy. Dichotomising at personal medians loses dose information and may classify similar days differently. Reactivity to diaries may alter scrolling, while compliance and attrition can bias results. Same-day measurement does not prove whether exposure preceded appraisal, and daily events or mood may confound the contrast. The small convenience sample limits power and generalisability. Future research could use more participants, timestamped prompts, consensual device summaries, and multilevel modelling of continuous exposure with lagged outcomes.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Choosing mean instead of median cut-points after seeing results, dropping low-compliance participants selectively, or reporting only same-day rather than lagged contrasts could produce a stronger pessimism story. Such analytic flexibility should be constrained by preregistration.
