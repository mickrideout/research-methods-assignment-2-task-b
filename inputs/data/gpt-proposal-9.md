## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian Instagram users aged 18–30 who own no home, more frequent upward comparison with peers’ housing achievements will be associated with higher housing-worry scores.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among Australian Instagram users aged 18–30 who own no home, upward comparison with peers’ housing achievements will not be associated with housing-worry scores.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Predictor:** mean frequency of five upward housing-comparison behaviours. **Outcome:** six-item housing-worry mean. **Confounders:** general anxiety, income, relationship status, parental assistance, current tenure, and daily Instagram time.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional Qualtrics survey will recruit 140 Australian residents aged 18–30 who use Instagram weekly and own no home. University and community notices plus AUD 180 advertising support recruitment. Items distinguish upward comparison from simple exposure, informed by social-comparison theory (Festinger, 1954) and social-media evidence (Vogel et al., 2014). The survey also uses unmodified GAD-7 (Spitzer et al., 2006) and measures confounders. Ten AUD 40 vouchers yield about AUD 580 cost and 70 hours. Participants consent anonymously, may skip sensitive financial items, receive support links, and provide no handles, screenshots, or private content.

## 5. Statistical Tests

Pearson correlation will first quantify the linear association between two approximately continuous composite scores. A scatterplot will assess linearity and influential outliers; Q–Q plots will assess approximate bivariate normality. If the relationship is monotonic but markedly non-normal, Spearman correlation will replace Pearson’s r. The primary adjusted analysis will be multiple linear regression with comparison frequency as the focal predictor and general anxiety, income, relationship status, parental assistance, tenure, and Instagram time as covariates. Regression assumptions include independent observations, linearity, normal and homoscedastic residuals, low multicollinearity, and no influential cases. At α = .05, r, the adjusted slope, 95% confidence intervals, and adjusted R² will be reported. Neither analysis establishes that comparison causes worry.

## 6. Limitations

Housing-specific comparison and worry items need cognitive piloting and reliability assessment. Recall and social-desirability biases may affect both measures, and anxious participants may notice comparison cues more readily. Instagram-only eligibility excludes non-users and limits platform generalisation. Financial resources are difficult to capture through brief income and parental-assistance items, leaving residual confounding. Cross-sectional measurement cannot establish direction. A future experience-sampling study could prompt participants after naturally occurring use and model whether comparison episodes precede short-term changes in worry.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Analysing a single emotionally loaded item, excluding affluent respondents, or omitting general anxiety could make comparison appear uniquely harmful. Calling the remaining coefficient causal would ignore reverse direction and residual wealth differences. These are selective, not defensible, interpretations.
