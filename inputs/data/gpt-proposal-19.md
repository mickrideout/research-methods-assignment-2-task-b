## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, higher baseline doomscrolling will predict higher housing-anxiety scores four weeks later after adjustment for baseline housing anxiety and socioeconomic factors.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, baseline doomscrolling will not predict four-week housing anxiety after adjustment for baseline housing anxiety and the specified socioeconomic factors.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Predictor:** baseline Doomscrolling Scale total. **Outcome:** four-week six-item housing-anxiety mean. **Confounders:** baseline housing anxiety, income, employment security, housing costs, major housing events, and general anxiety.

## 4. Data Collection

A two-wave online panel will recruit 90 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home and use social media. University and renter networks plus AUD 150 advertising recruit Wave 1; automated reminders support Wave 2 after four weeks. Both waves measure housing anxiety; baseline also uses the Doomscrolling Scale (Satici et al., 2023), GAD-7 (Spitzer et al., 2006), and confounders, while follow-up records major events. Seventy completers receive AUD 10, setting maximum costs near AUD 900 and workload 90 hours. Consent, coded linkage, separately stored contacts, distress resources, withdrawal, and deletion of payment details protect participants.

## 5. Statistical Tests

Multiple linear regression will predict continuous follow-up housing anxiety from baseline doomscrolling while controlling baseline housing anxiety, income, employment security, housing costs, major events, and general anxiety. Baseline outcome adjustment makes the doomscrolling coefficient an estimate of association with later anxiety conditional on starting level, matching the temporal hypothesis. Diagnostics assess independent observations, linearity, normal and homoscedastic residuals, multicollinearity, and influential cases; bootstrap confidence intervals will address persistent residual non-normality. Attrition will be examined by comparing baseline characteristics of completers and non-completers, with complete-case conclusions qualified accordingly. At α = .05, coefficients, 95% confidence intervals, semipartial R², and adjusted R² will be reported. Temporal order strengthens evidence but unrandomised exposure still prevents causal claims.

## 6. Limitations

Four weeks may be too short for meaningful perceptual change yet long enough for attrition. Self-report creates common-method bias, and the modest sample limits complex adjustment. Baseline anxiety may be measured with error, while unobserved news events or personality could confound the lagged association. Completers may be unusually conscientious or worried. A future larger three-wave panel could use validated housing-anxiety measures, consensual device summaries, inverse-probability attrition weights, and cross-lagged analyses to examine reciprocal direction.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Omitting baseline anxiety, ignoring selective attrition, or presenting temporal prediction as proof of causation could make doomscrolling appear more damaging. Choosing only the follow-up interval with the strongest coefficient would add outcome-selection bias. All waves must be reported.
