## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian Instagram users aged 18–30 who own no home, predominantly passive housing-content users will have a larger gap between desired and expected home-purchase age than predominantly active users.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among eligible Australian Instagram users, the mean gap between desired and expected home-purchase age will not differ between predominantly passive and predominantly active housing-content users.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** predominant housing-content use, passive viewing or active posting/commenting. **Dependent variable:** expected purchase age minus desired purchase age. **Confounders:** current age, income, savings, parental assistance, relationship status, and housing-content frequency.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional survey will recruit 160 Australian residents aged 18–30 who use Instagram for housing content and own no home. University, renter, and first-home-buyer groups plus AUD 200 advertising recruit participants. Behaviour-frequency items classify use at a preregistered passive-to-active ratio; respondents state desired and expected purchase ages, framed by AHURI research (Parkinson et al., 2019), and confounders. Active/passive categories require contextual interpretation (Verduyn et al., 2022). Twelve AUD 40 voucher draws yield about AUD 680 cost and 75 hours. Consent, anonymity, optional finance items, range checks without forced answers, and no handles or private posts are required.

## 5. Statistical Tests

An independent-samples t-test will compare mean expectation gaps across passive-dominant and active-dominant groups. It fits two independent, self-selected categorical groups and one continuous difference outcome. Independence, groupwise normality, influential outliers, and equal variances will be assessed; Welch’s t-test addresses unequal variances and Mann–Whitney U severe non-normality. Supplementary multiple regression will retain the continuous passive-to-active ratio and adjust for age, income, savings, parental assistance, relationship status, and exposure frequency, reducing information loss from classification. At α = .05, group means, the mean difference, 95% confidence interval, and Hedges’ g will be reported. The comparison is associational: existing expectations may influence use style, and active versus passive use is not uniformly beneficial or harmful.

## 6. Limitations

Dichotomising mixed behaviours can misclassify users and obscure beneficial passive or harmful active experiences. Desired and expected ages may be uncertain, and the gap mechanically depends on current age. Instagram-only convenience recruitment restricts generalisation. Wealth and future family plans are imperfectly measured, leaving confounding; cross-sectional data cannot determine whether use style preceded expectations. Future work could use repeated behavioural diaries and aspiration measures, analyse active and passive subtypes separately, and follow actual housing milestones.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Changing the passive–active ratio after seeing results, removing respondents expecting never to buy, or ignoring savings could make passive viewing appear fatalistic. Treating all passive use as harmful would also contradict the construct’s nuance and encourage selective reporting.
