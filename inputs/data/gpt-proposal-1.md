## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australians aged 18–30 who do not own a home, more weekly minutes spent passively viewing property listings and real-estate posts on Instagram will be associated with lower perceived ability to afford a suitable first home.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among Australians aged 18–30 who do not own a home, weekly minutes spent passively viewing Instagram property content will not be associated with perceived ability to afford a suitable first home.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Predictor:** self-reported weekly passive property-content minutes. **Outcome:** mean of five 1–7 perceived-affordability items. **Confounders:** personal income, employment security, city versus regional residence, parental homeownership, and current housing costs.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional Qualtrics survey will recruit 120 Australian residents aged 18–30 who use Instagram and own no residential property. University notices, community groups, and AUD 180 targeted advertising will recruit participants. Measures cover exposure minutes, five pilot-tested affordability items, and confounders; ABS and AHURI findings inform item content (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2022; Parkinson et al., 2019). Ten AUD 40 vouchers bring costs to about AUD 580; workload is approximately 65 hours. Consent, withdrawal information, anonymous responses, Australian data storage, and no collection of handles or private posts protect participants.

## 5. Statistical Tests

The primary analysis will use multiple linear regression because the predictor is continuous, the outcome is an approximately interval mean score, and confounders can enter the same model. The exposure coefficient tests the directional hypothesis while adjusting for income, employment security, residence, parental homeownership, and housing costs. Diagnostics will assess independent observations, linearity, normally distributed and homoscedastic residuals, multicollinearity, and influential cases. If residual assumptions remain poor after defensible transformation, percentile bootstrap confidence intervals will be reported. Internal consistency of the five outcome items will be checked before averaging. Tests use α = .05; the coefficient, 95% confidence interval, adjusted R², and semipartial R² will be reported. The cross-sectional coefficient will be described as an association, not an effect.

## 6. Limitations

Convenience recruitment may overrepresent university-educated and housing-engaged Instagram users. Recalled minutes are imprecise, and the new affordability items require pilot testing rather than being assumed valid. Reverse direction is plausible: people already worried about affordability may seek more property posts. Current market conditions and unmeasured wealth could produce residual confounding. The cross-sectional design therefore cannot establish temporal order or causation. A future probability-based panel could combine repeated perceptions with participant-exported screen-time summaries, subject to consent, to improve representativeness, measurement, and temporal evidence.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Analysts could misleadingly claim that Instagram creates pessimism by omitting income and housing costs, deleting low-exposure cases, or highlighting significance while suppressing a trivial effect size. Such selective analysis would not overcome reverse causation and is not recommended practice.
