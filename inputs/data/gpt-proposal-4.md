## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, the association between following property influencers and expected age at first-home purchase will differ by gender, with a larger follower–non-follower gap among women than men.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

There will be no interaction between property-influencer following status and gender in expected first-home purchase age among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variables:** property-influencer following, yes/no, and self-described gender, women/men. **Dependent variable:** expected purchase age. **Confounders:** current age, income, savings, parental financial support, relationship status, and metropolitan residence. Other gender identities remain eligible but are analysed descriptively if sparse.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional Qualtrics survey will recruit 180 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home, aiming for balanced follower-by-gender cells. Recruitment uses university channels, community groups, and AUD 250 social advertising. Measures include verified screening, following status, expected purchase age, and confounders; housing-aspiration wording draws on Parkinson et al. (2019). Twelve AUD 40 voucher draws yield about AUD 730 total cost and 80 researcher hours. Participants consent anonymously; no influencer handles, private posts, or unnecessary identifiers are collected, and small gender groups are protected from disclosure.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A 2 × 2 factorial ANOVA will test the influencer-following main effect, gender main effect, and prespecified interaction on continuous expected purchase age. The interaction directly addresses whether the follower–non-follower difference varies by gender. Independent observations, approximately normal residuals, homogeneous cell variances, and influential outliers will be assessed. Welch-type robust inference will be considered for heteroscedasticity; severe skew will prompt a rank-based factorial sensitivity analysis. A supplementary multiple regression with product terms will adjust for age, income, savings, parental support, relationship status, and residence. At α = .05, estimated marginal means, 95% confidence intervals, and partial η² will be reported. Following and gender are not randomised, so all differences and interactions are associational.

## 6. Limitations

Balancing cells through quotas does not create representative groups, and binary focal categories simplify gender diversity. Following status poorly captures dose, content quality, cross-platform exposure, or social-comparison responses (Vogel et al., 2014). Purchase-age expectations may be uncertain or censored beyond the response range. Self-selection is substantial: financially ambitious or pessimistic people may follow influencers. Covariate adjustment cannot remove unmeasured wealth or aspiration differences, and cross-sectional data cannot establish direction. A future panel should repeatedly measure actual content exposure, changing savings, and expectations while oversampling gender-diverse participants for adequately powered comparisons.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Testing many gender codings, reporting only the largest subgroup contrast, or omitting savings and parental support could make an unstable interaction appear persuasive. Recasting it as influencer causation would compound the problem. Transparent categories and full interaction results are essential.
