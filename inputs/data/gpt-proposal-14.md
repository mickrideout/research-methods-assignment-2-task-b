## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, trust in housing information from social-media creators will be associated with whether the Australian housing system is judged mostly fair or mostly unfair.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, trust category for social-media housing creators will be independent of the binary judgement that Australia’s housing system is mostly fair or unfair.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** creator trust, low or high using a prespecified scale midpoint. **Dependent variable:** housing-system fairness, mostly fair or unfair. **Confounders:** political ideology, income, tenure, parental homeownership, institutional trust, and housing-content exposure.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional survey will recruit 200 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home. Recruitment uses university lists, renter groups, and AUD 250 advertising. Four trust items are classified at the neutral midpoint; one forced-choice item records fairness, alongside confounders. Housing-cost context comes from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (2022); misinformation motivates source-trust items (Vosoughi et al., 2018). Fifteen AUD 40 voucher draws make costs about AUD 850 and workload 80 hours. Consent explains categorical questions, “prefer not to say” remains available elsewhere, responses are anonymous, cells are disclosure-checked, and no private accounts are accessed.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A chi-square test of independence will examine the 2 × 2 table of creator-trust category by housing-system fairness. Both variables are categorical, observations are independent, and the hypothesis concerns association rather than a mean difference. Expected cell counts will be inspected; Fisher’s exact test will replace chi-square if any expected count is too small. Pearson’s χ², two-sided α = .05, cell percentages, 95% confidence interval for the odds ratio, and φ will be reported. A supplementary binary logistic regression will adjust the fairness outcome for ideology, income, tenure, parental homeownership, institutional trust, and exposure. The midpoint rule and analysis will be preregistered to avoid outcome-driven categorisation. Cross-sectional association cannot establish that creator trust changes fairness perceptions.

## 6. Limitations

Dichotomising trust and fairness sacrifices nuance and can reduce power. A forced choice may misclassify ambivalent respondents, while “creator” includes diverse sources. Common-method self-report and non-probability recruitment can bias the table. Ideology and institutional trust may not be fully captured, and reverse direction is plausible: perceived unfairness may shape which creators seem trustworthy. Future work should retain continuous validated measures and use a randomised source-cue experiment that presents identical housing information under creator and institutional labels.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Moving the trust cut-point after inspecting cell counts, excluding neutral respondents, or reporting an unadjusted odds ratio while hiding ideology adjustment could manufacture a polarisation story. Prespecified thresholds and complete reporting are needed to resist selective analysis.
