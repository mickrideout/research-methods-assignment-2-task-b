## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, those classified as predominantly passive Instagram users will report a larger homeownership expectation gap—the difference between aspired and realistically expected purchase age—than predominantly active users.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

There is no difference in the homeownership expectation gap between predominantly passive and predominantly active Instagram users among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**IV:** predominant use style (passive vs active, dichotomised). **DV:** expectation gap (aspired minus expected purchase age, years). **Confounders:** household income, daily Instagram use, and parental home-ownership.

## 4. Data Collection

Cross-sectional online survey of 130 non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, recruited via university networks and AUD $160 of Instagram-targeted advertising. A 10-minute survey administers a use-style scale adapted from active/passive social media research (Verduyn et al., 2022), aspired and expected purchase-age items, income, daily use, and parental home-ownership. Participants enter a draw for five AUD $80 vouchers (AUD $400), keeping total cost near AUD $560. Online consent, voluntary participation, and anonymised storage meet standard ethics requirements.

## 5. Statistical Tests

An independent-samples t-test compares mean expectation-gap scores between passive-dominant and active-dominant users, matching a two-group categorical predictor with a continuous, approximately normal outcome. Income, daily use, and parental home-ownership are entered as covariates in a supplementary ANCOVA. Assumptions include independence, normality of residuals within groups (Shapiro-Wilk), and homogeneity of variance (Levene's test); Welch's t-test is reported if variances differ. Significance is set at α = .05, with a 95% confidence interval for the mean difference and Cohen's d as the effect size. As use style is self-selected, results describe an association, not a causal effect of passive use.

## 6. Limitations

Dichotomising a continuous use-style measure discards information (Verduyn et al., 2022). Non-probability sampling limits generalisability. The cross-sectional design cannot establish whether use style precedes or follows expectation gaps. Hypothetical expectation measures may not predict actual purchase timing (Parkinson et al., 2019). A future longitudinal design tracking actual outcomes would strengthen validity.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

A significant difference could be reframed as proof passive scrolling 'causes' pessimism by omitting income as a confounder or emphasising significance over the effect size. Reporting only participants with extreme use-style scores would inflate the difference—a risk of selective analysis, not sound practice.
