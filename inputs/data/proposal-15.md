## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, preferred housing-content format (short-form video versus long-form video/podcast) will be associated with categorical level of housing-policy knowledge (low, medium, high), with long-form preference linked to higher knowledge.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

There is no association between preferred housing-content format (short-form vs long-form) and categorical housing-policy knowledge level (low/medium/high) among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**IV:** preferred content format (short-form vs long-form, categorical). **DV:** housing-policy knowledge level (low/medium/high, quiz-derived). **Confounders:** education level, general news consumption, and age within range.

## 4. Data Collection

Cross-sectional online survey of 150 non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, recruited via university networks, podcast and TikTok-adjacent community groups, and AUD $170 of advertising. A 12-minute survey records format preference, a 10-item housing-policy knowledge quiz (categorised into tertiles), education, general news consumption, and age. Participants enter a draw for six AUD $75 vouchers (AUD $450), keeping total cost near AUD $620. Online consent, voluntary participation, and anonymised storage meet standard ethics requirements.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A chi-square test of independence examines the association between format preference (two levels) and knowledge tertile (three levels), appropriate for two categorical variables with an outcome of more than two levels. Education, news consumption, and age are examined via a supplementary ordinal logistic regression to adjust for confounding. Assumptions include independence of observations and expected cell counts of at least five in each of the six cells. If expected counts are too low, categories are collapsed to low/high knowledge and Fisher's exact test is used instead. Significance is set at α = .05, with Cramér's V as the effect size and odds ratios from the ordinal model. As format is self-selected, results describe an association, not a causal effect of format on knowledge.

## 6. Limitations

Self-selected format preference likely reflects pre-existing interest and general media literacy, not format alone. The knowledge quiz is a study-specific, non-validated measure. Non-probability sampling limits generalisability. Residual confounding from general education attainment may persist despite adjustment. A future study experimentally assigning matched-content formats would isolate format-specific effects on retained knowledge.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

A significant chi-square could be reframed as proof short-form content 'causes' poor knowledge by omitting education as a confounder or reporting only the extreme knowledge tertiles. Collapsing categories differently could manufacture a stronger-looking pattern—a risk of selective analysis, not sound practice.
