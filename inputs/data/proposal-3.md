## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, randomised exposure to a five-minute doomscrolling-style TikTok compilation of negative housing-crisis content will cause higher immediate state anxiety than exposure to a neutral lifestyle-content compilation of equal length.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

There is no difference in mean post-exposure state anxiety between Australians aged 18–30 randomly assigned to view negative housing-crisis TikTok content versus neutral lifestyle content; any observed difference reflects chance variation.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**IV:** experimental condition (negative housing content vs neutral content, randomly assigned). **DV:** post-exposure state anxiety (0–100 visual analogue scale). **Covariates:** baseline anxiety, prior housing-content exposure, current living situation.

## 4. Data Collection

Randomised online experiment with 80 non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, recruited via university lists and paid social-media ads (AUD $200), randomly assigned via Qualtrics randomiser to one of two five-minute video compilations. Baseline anxiety is measured pre-exposure; state anxiety is measured immediately post-exposure using a single-item VAS plus a validated short state-anxiety item set. Participants receive a AUD $10 e-voucher each (AUD $800 total), kept within budget by using existing public-domain and creator-permitted clips. Debriefing, informed consent, and the right to withdraw are provided given the mildly distressing content.

## 5. Statistical Tests

Given the expected right-skew of state-anxiety scores in a modest two-group experimental sample, a Mann-Whitney U test compares distributions between conditions, appropriate for one categorical predictor (two independent randomised groups) and one ordinal/non-normal continuous outcome. Baseline anxiety and prior exposure are checked for balance across conditions using chi-square and t-tests. Assumptions include independence of observations and similarity of distribution shape across groups; if distributions are similar in shape, medians are compared directly. Significance is set at α = .05, with a Hodges-Lehmann estimate, 95% confidence interval, and rank-biserial correlation as the effect size. Random assignment supports a causal interpretation of any observed difference.

## 6. Limitations

Artificial, brief laboratory-style exposure may not replicate cumulative real-world doomscrolling (Satici et al., 2023). Convenience sampling limits generalisability. Single-item state-anxiety measurement has limited validity compared to multi-item clinical scales. Demand characteristics may inflate reported distress. A future study could use a longer, more ecologically valid exposure period with physiological anxiety measures to corroborate self-report.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

A significant result could be overstated as proof housing content 'causes' lasting harm by ignoring the brief, single-exposure manipulation and omitting the baseline-anxiety covariate. Reporting only participants with the largest pre-post change would exaggerate the effect—a risk of selective analysis, not recommended practice.
