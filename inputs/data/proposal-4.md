## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, following property/real-estate influencers on social media and gender will jointly predict expected age of first home purchase, with followers expecting later purchase ages than non-followers, particularly among women.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

There is no main effect of following property influencers, no main effect of gender, and no interaction between them on expected age of first home purchase among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**IVs:** influencer-following status (yes/no) and gender (man/woman), both categorical. **DV:** expected age of first home purchase (years, continuous). **Confounders:** household income, relationship status, and current savings rate.

## 4. Data Collection

Cross-sectional online survey of 160 non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30 (balanced across gender and following status), recruited via university channels, property-related Facebook/Reddit groups, and AUD $200 of advertising. A 10-minute survey records influencer-following status, gender, expected purchase age, income, relationship status, and savings rate. Participants enter a draw for six AUD $75 vouchers (AUD $450), keeping total cost under AUD $700. Online consent, voluntary participation, and anonymised responses meet standard ethics requirements; no influencer accounts are contacted or scraped.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A 2×2 factorial ANOVA (following status × gender) tests main and interaction effects on expected purchase age, appropriate for two categorical predictors and one continuous, normally distributed outcome. Income, relationship status, and savings rate are tested as covariates in a follow-up ANCOVA. Assumptions include independence of observations, normality of residuals within cells (Shapiro-Wilk), and homogeneity of variance across cells (Levene's test). If assumptions are violated, an aligned rank transform ANOVA is used as a non-parametric fallback. Significance is set at α = .05, with partial eta-squared reported as the effect size for each effect, alongside 95% confidence intervals for cell means. As groups are self-selected, findings describe associations, not causal influencer effects.

## 6. Limitations

Self-selected following status confounds exposure with prior housing pessimism or interest. Non-probability sampling limits generalisability across the diverse Australian gender-diverse population, which this design collapses into a binary measure. Self-reported expectations are hypothetical and may not predict actual behaviour (Parkinson et al., 2019). Unequal cell sizes could reduce power. A future longitudinal design tracking actual purchase timing would strengthen validity.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

A significant interaction could be reframed as proof influencers 'cause' delayed expectations in women by omitting income and savings covariates, or by reporting the interaction term while ignoring its small effect size. Dropping non-binary respondents would further bias conclusions—a risk of selective analysis, not recommended practice.
