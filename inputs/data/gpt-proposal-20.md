## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30 randomly assigned to housing posts with source-and-context labels will classify more claims accurately than participants shown identical posts without labels.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Mean housing-claim accuracy will not differ between eligible participants randomly assigned to posts with source-and-context labels and identical unlabelled posts.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** randomised label condition, contextual labels or none. **Dependent variable:** number of ten housing claims correctly classified. Randomisation addresses confounding; potential precision covariates are education, news literacy, political ideology, prior issue knowledge, and social-media use.

## 4. Data Collection

An online randomised experiment will recruit 140 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home. University, library, and renter-group posts plus AUD 180 advertising recruit participants. They view ten simulated posts containing true and false housing claims checked against authoritative sources (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2022; Stone et al., 2020); one group sees source-and-context labels. Participants judge each claim, then receive a corrective debrief. False information can diffuse widely online (Vosoughi et al., 2018). Twelve AUD 50 voucher draws produce about AUD 780 cost and 85 hours. Consent, anonymity, debriefing, and no private-content access apply.

## 5. Statistical Tests

An independent-samples t-test will compare mean accuracy totals, scored 0–10, between randomised label groups. This matches two independent conditions and a near-continuous summed outcome. Independence, groupwise distribution shape, equal variances, ceiling effects, and influential values will be checked; Welch’s test addresses unequal variances and Mann–Whitney U severe non-normality. A supplementary ANCOVA will adjust for prespecified education, news literacy, ideology, prior knowledge, and social-media use to improve precision, not repair randomisation. At α = .05, group means, the mean difference, 95% confidence interval, and Hedges’ g will be reported. Random assignment supports a causal claim about immediate classification accuracy for these labels and claims, but not enduring misinformation resistance or all platform labelling.

## 6. Limitations

Researcher-created posts lack real accounts, engagement metrics, and personalisation. Accuracy totals may show ceiling effects, while a single exposure cannot measure lasting belief correction or sharing. Labels combine source and context, preventing identification of the active component. Participants may search externally unless instructed not to, and convenience recruitment limits representativeness. A future factorial study could separate source from context labels, delay retesting, record confidence and sharing intentions, and use a broader stratified sample.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Dropping difficult claims, scoring “unsure” as correct in only the labelled group, or highlighting accuracy while hiding unchanged confidence could make labels seem universally effective. Generalising beyond tested claims would also be selective. Scoring and outcomes require preregistration.
