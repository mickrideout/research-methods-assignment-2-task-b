## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, preference for short-form video over long-form housing explainers will be associated with lower objective knowledge of current Australian housing-policy mechanisms.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, preferred housing-content format will be independent of objective housing-policy knowledge category.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** preferred format, short-form video or long-form explainer. **Dependent variable:** policy knowledge, lower or higher using a prespecified quiz threshold. **Confounders:** education, political interest, news use, age, English proficiency, and total housing-content exposure.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional online survey will recruit 200 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home and consume housing information socially. University, library, and youth-group notices plus AUD 200 advertising recruit participants. They choose their usual format and complete ten fact-checked multiple-choice items on policy mechanisms, not party positions; item sources and scoring are preregistered. Twelve AUD 50 voucher draws produce about AUD 800 cost and 85 hours. Consent, anonymity, “unsure” options, accessible language, secure storage, and no account access apply. AHURI material supports the policy-item background (Stone et al., 2020).

## 5. Statistical Tests

A chi-square test of independence will test association between two format categories and two knowledge categories. It matches a 2 × 2 design with independent observations and categorical variables. The higher-knowledge threshold, set before collection at seven correct answers, will not be tuned to results. Expected cell counts will be checked; Fisher’s exact test is the fallback for sparse cells. Pearson’s χ², α = .05, row percentages, φ, and a 95% confidence interval for the odds ratio will be reported. Supplementary logistic regression will adjust for education, political interest, news use, age, English proficiency, and exposure. A sensitivity analysis will use continuous quiz scores in multiple regression to show whether dichotomisation drives conclusions. Preference is self-selected, so association cannot demonstrate that short-form content reduces knowledge.

## 6. Limitations

Format preference does not measure actual viewing, attention, source quality, or message complexity. Misinformation can diffuse widely (Vosoughi et al., 2018). The policy quiz may privilege highly educated or English-proficient participants and can become outdated as policy changes. Dichotomising scores loses information. Convenience recruitment and self-selection limit generalisability, while knowledgeable participants may choose long-form content, reversing the proposed direction. Future research could randomise equivalent short and long explanations, immediately test comprehension and delayed retention, and update items through expert review.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Lowering the “high knowledge” threshold until significance appears, deleting “unsure” responses, or ignoring education could depict short videos as inherently misleading. Reporting only categorical results while suppressing a null continuous-score analysis would be selective and unacceptable.
