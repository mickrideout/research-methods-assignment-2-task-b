## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, mean perceived housing affordability will differ across those who primarily encounter housing content on TikTok, Instagram, and Facebook.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, mean perceived housing affordability will be equal across TikTok-, Instagram-, and Facebook-primary housing-content groups.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** primary housing-content platform, TikTok, Instagram, or Facebook. **Dependent variable:** five-item perceived-affordability mean. **Confounders:** age, income, metropolitan residence, total exposure time, content valence, and parental homeownership.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional survey will recruit 180 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home and encounter housing content weekly, targeting 60 per platform. University networks, renter groups, and AUD 250 platform advertisements support quotas. Measures cover primary platform, five pilot-tested affordability items, exposure, valence, and confounders; ABS and AHURI evidence frame the questions (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2022; Parkinson et al., 2019). Twelve AUD 40 voucher draws produce about AUD 730 cost and 80 hours. Participants consent anonymously; duplicate checks avoid identifiable data, sensitive financial items are optional, and no private content is requested.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A one-way between-groups ANOVA will compare the continuous affordability composite across three independent platform groups. The omnibus F test matches the nondirectional hypothesis that at least one platform mean differs while limiting inflation from three initial pairwise tests. Independence, within-group residual normality, homogeneity of variance, and influential outliers will be checked. Welch’s ANOVA with Games–Howell comparisons will replace conventional ANOVA and Tukey comparisons if variances differ; Kruskal–Wallis is reserved for severe distributional violations. A supplementary regression with platform indicators will adjust for age, income, residence, exposure time, valence, and parental homeownership. At α = .05, group means, 95% confidence intervals, ω², and multiplicity-adjusted pairwise differences will be reported. Platform self-selection means differences are associative, not causal.

## 6. Limitations

Primary-platform categories ignore multi-platform use and within-platform feed differences. Quotas improve cell sizes but not representativeness, and Facebook users may differ systematically in age or circumstances. Self-reported exposure and valence are imprecise; new affordability items need psychometric evaluation. Covariate adjustment cannot remove algorithmic history, housing interest, or wealth differences. Cross-sectional data do not establish direction. A future matched-content experiment could randomise platform-style interfaces while holding messages constant, separating presentation effects from user selection and content composition.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Reporting only the largest pairwise contrast after a nonsignificant omnibus test, switching platform categories post hoc, or omitting content valence could brand one platform as harmful. Such cherry-picking would exploit multiple comparisons and cannot establish a platform effect.
