## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30 randomly assigned to positive, realistic housing pathways posted in a simulated feed will report higher immediate homeownership optimism than those assigned structurally pessimistic housing narratives.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Immediate homeownership optimism will not differ between eligible participants randomly assigned to positive-pathway and structurally pessimistic housing-feed narratives.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** randomised narrative valence, positive-pathway or pessimistic. **Dependent variable:** post-exposure six-item optimism mean. Randomisation addresses confounding; potential precision covariates are baseline optimism, income, savings, parental assistance, and current tenure.

## 4. Data Collection

An online randomised experiment will recruit 110 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home. University and community notices plus AUD 150 advertising support recruitment. Participants complete baseline optimism, view six matched researcher-created posts, then repeat optimism and complete a valence check. Narratives use realistic pathways and constraints reflected in AHURI evidence (Stone et al., 2020), without fabricated testimonials. Ten AUD 50 voucher draws bring costs to AUD 650 and workload to about 75 hours. Consent, anonymity, distress information, debriefing, and withdrawal before submission are provided.

## 5. Statistical Tests

ANCOVA will compare post-exposure optimism between randomised valence groups while adjusting for baseline optimism, improving precision and accounting for chance imbalance. The condition coefficient tests the hypothesis for one binary independent variable and a continuous outcome. Assumptions are independent observations, linear covariate–outcome association, homogeneous regression slopes, approximately normal and homoscedastic residuals, and no influential cases. If slope homogeneity fails, the condition-by-baseline interaction will be reported; robust standard errors will address heteroscedasticity. A change-score independent t-test will provide a transparent sensitivity analysis. At α = .05, adjusted means, the condition difference, 95% confidence interval, and partial η² will be reported. Causality is limited to the immediate effect of these matched narratives, not long-term optimism or social media generally.

## 6. Limitations

The contrast combines narrative valence with different substantive information, so the active ingredient may be facts, tone, or exemplars. Social-comparison responses may vary individually (Vogel et al., 2014). A brief simulated feed may create demand characteristics and cannot reproduce repeated algorithmic exposure. The new optimism measure requires validation, and convenience sampling limits representativeness. The design assesses an immediate response rather than durable expectations or behaviour. A future factorial experiment could independently manipulate factual constraint information and emotional tone, use delayed follow-up, and recruit a demographically stratified sample.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Ignoring a failed valence check, removing pessimistic-condition outliers only, or describing short-lived optimism as increased home-buying capacity would overstate the intervention. Selecting whichever optimism item reaches significance is another selective practice; the preregistered composite should govern.
