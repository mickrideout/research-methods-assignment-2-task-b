## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, mean perceived housing affordability will differ significantly across groups defined by their primary platform for housing-related content: Instagram, TikTok, or Facebook.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

There is no difference in mean perceived housing affordability across non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30 who primarily consume housing-related content on Instagram, TikTok, or Facebook.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**IV:** primary platform for housing content (Instagram/TikTok/Facebook, categorical). **DV:** perceived housing affordability (7-point scale). **Confounders:** age within range, household income, and time spent on social media daily.

## 4. Data Collection

Cross-sectional online survey of 150 non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30 (50 per platform group), recruited via university networks and AUD $220 of platform-targeted advertising. A 9-minute survey records primary-platform group, the affordability-perception scale, age, income, and daily social-media minutes. Participants enter a draw for six AUD $70 vouchers (AUD $420), keeping total cost near AUD $640. Informed online consent, voluntary participation, and anonymised storage meet standard ethics requirements.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A one-way between-groups ANOVA compares mean affordability perception across the three platform groups, appropriate for one categorical predictor with three independent levels and one continuous, approximately normal outcome. Age, income, and daily use are tested as covariates in a follow-up ANCOVA. Assumptions include independence of observations, normality of residuals within groups (Shapiro-Wilk), and homogeneity of variance (Levene's test). If variances differ, Welch's ANOVA with Games-Howell post hoc tests is used instead of Tukey's HSD. Significance is set at α = .05, with eta-squared as the effect size and 95% confidence intervals for group means. Because platform is self-selected, results describe an association, not a causal platform effect.

## 6. Limitations

Self-selected platform groups may differ systematically in prior housing attitudes, not just content exposure. Non-probability sampling limits generalisability (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2021). The cross-sectional design cannot establish temporal precedence. Unequal engagement levels within platform groups add residual confounding. A future study assessing content type directly, rather than platform label alone, would sharpen the comparison.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

A significant omnibus result could be reframed as proof one platform is uniquely harmful by reporting only the largest pairwise contrast while ignoring the small overall eta-squared. Dropping the smallest group would further bias comparisons—a risk of selective analysis, not sound practice.
