## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30 randomly assigned to view a five-minute feed of verified negative housing-crisis posts will show a larger pre-to-post increase in state housing anxiety than those viewing neutral Australian lifestyle posts.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

The mean pre-to-post change in state housing-anxiety scores will not differ between eligible participants randomly assigned to negative housing-crisis posts and neutral lifestyle posts.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Independent variable:** randomised feed condition, negative housing or neutral lifestyle. **Dependent variable:** pre-to-post change on six state housing-anxiety items. Randomisation addresses confounding; measured potential covariates are income, baseline general anxiety, housing costs, and prior housing-content exposure.

## 4. Data Collection

An online randomised experiment will recruit 100 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home. University and community posts plus AUD 150 advertising will direct participants to concealed allocation. They complete baseline measures including GAD-7 (Spitzer et al., 2006), view researcher-created posts without identifiable accounts, then repeat state housing-anxiety items. Content will be pilot-matched for length and readability. Ten AUD 50 voucher draws make total costs about AUD 700 and workload about 75 hours. Consent warns of distress; debriefing, support links, anonymity, and withdrawal options apply.

## 5. Statistical Tests

An independent-samples t-test will compare change scores between randomised groups, appropriately testing one categorical, between-participants manipulation against one continuous outcome. Random allocation supports a causal inference about this brief exposure if allocation, compliance, and attrition remain sound. Independence, change-score normality, influential outliers, and homogeneity of variance will be checked; Welch’s test will address unequal variances, and Mann–Whitney U will be used for severe non-normality. A sensitivity ANCOVA predicting post-score from condition and baseline score will improve precision and examine consistency, with prespecified covariates added only if meaningful imbalance occurs. At α = .05, the mean difference, 95% confidence interval, and Hedges’ g will be reported. Conclusions will be limited to immediate state anxiety, not lasting mental-health harm.

## 6. Limitations

A five-minute simulated feed has limited ecological validity and cannot represent measured, repeated doomscrolling (Satici et al., 2023). Demand characteristics may reveal the hypothesis, while newly written state items have limited validation. Online participation reduces control over attention; manipulation checks may themselves cue responses. A sample of 100 may miss small effects and will not represent all young Australian non-homeowners. Replication should use a preregistered multi-day field experiment with consensual screen-time summaries, repeated validated state measures, and a broader recruitment frame.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Dropping inattentive participants after seeing condition-specific results, analysing post-scores without baseline, or calling immediate discomfort lasting psychological damage could manufacture a stronger harm narrative. Those researcher degrees of freedom require preregistration and transparent sensitivity analyses, not selective reporting.
