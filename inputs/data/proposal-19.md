## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, baseline doomscrolling frequency will positively predict housing-related anxiety six weeks later, after statistically controlling for baseline anxiety, income, and employment status.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

After controlling for baseline anxiety, income, and employment status, baseline doomscrolling frequency does not predict housing-related anxiety six weeks later among non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**IV:** baseline doomscrolling frequency (Doomscrolling Scale, continuous). **DV:** Wave 3 housing-related anxiety (adapted GAD-7, continuous). **Confounders:** baseline anxiety, household income, and employment status.

## 4. Data Collection

Three-wave online panel with 45 non-home-owning Australians aged 18–30, recruited via university networks and AUD $150 of advertising, surveyed at baseline, three weeks, and six weeks. Each 6-minute wave administers the Doomscrolling Scale (Satici et al., 2023), the housing-adapted GAD-7 (Spitzer et al., 2006), and demographics at baseline only. Participants receive a AUD $6 voucher per completed wave, up to AUD $18 each (AUD $810 for 45 participants), keeping total cost at AUD $960.

## 5. Statistical Tests

A multiple linear regression predicts Wave 3 anxiety from baseline doomscrolling, controlling for baseline anxiety, income, and employment status, appropriate for a continuous outcome with continuous and categorical predictors in a lagged design. Assumptions include linearity, independence of residuals, homoscedasticity, normality of residuals, and absence of multicollinearity (VIF < 5). If normality is violated, a robust standard-error regression is used instead. Significance is set at α = .05, with 95% confidence intervals for each coefficient and semi-partial R² for doomscrolling as the effect size. Controlling for baseline anxiety strengthens temporal inference, but the absence of randomisation means results remain associational, not fully causal.

## 6. Limitations

Attrition across three waves may bias the retained sample toward more engaged participants. Self-report measures at each wave risk consistency bias. Six weeks may be too short to capture slower-forming attitude change (Australian Housing and Urban Research Institute, 2020). Residual confounding from unmeasured life events remains possible. A future study with more waves and objective screen-time data would strengthen causal inference.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

A significant lagged coefficient could be overstated as proof doomscrolling 'causes' rising anxiety by omitting baseline anxiety as a covariate or reporting only completers with the largest increases. This is a risk of selective analysis, not sound practice.
