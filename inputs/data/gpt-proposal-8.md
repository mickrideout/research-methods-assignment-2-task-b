## 1. Proposed Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, higher Doomscrolling Scale scores will be associated with stronger attribution of the housing crisis to older generations, after adjustment for socioeconomic and political factors.

## 2. Null Hypothesis

Among Australian non-homeowners aged 18–30, Doomscrolling Scale scores will not be associated with generational-blame scores after adjustment for the specified socioeconomic and political factors.

## 3. Independent, Dependent and Confounding Variables

**Predictor:** Doomscrolling Scale total. **Outcome:** mean agreement with five generational-blame statements. **Confounders:** age, income, political ideology, parental homeownership, perceived housing-cost burden, and weekly news exposure.

## 4. Data Collection

A cross-sectional online survey will recruit 160 Australian residents aged 18–30 who own no home and use social media. Recruitment uses university panels, renter groups, and AUD 220 advertising. The survey administers the validated Doomscrolling Scale (Satici et al., 2023), pilot-tested blame items informed by Australian housing research (Parkinson et al., 2019), and confounders. Twelve AUD 40 voucher draws make estimated costs AUD 700; data preparation and analysis keep workload near 75 hours. Consent explains potentially upsetting content, support links are supplied, responses are anonymous, optional demographic answers may be skipped, and no accounts or private posts are accessed.

## 5. Statistical Tests

Multiple linear regression will estimate the association between continuous doomscrolling and the approximately interval blame composite while adjusting for age, income, ideology, parental homeownership, cost burden, and news exposure. The focal slope directly tests whether blame scores rise with doomscrolling after those covariates are held constant. Assumptions include independent observations, linearity, normally distributed and homoscedastic residuals, low multicollinearity, and no influential cases. Nonlinearity will be explored with a prespecified quadratic sensitivity term; robust standard errors will address residual heteroscedasticity. Scale internal consistency will be assessed before forming the outcome. At α = .05, unstandardised and standardised coefficients, 95% confidence intervals, and adjusted R² will be reported. Cross-sectional self-report supports association only, not claims that doomscrolling creates blame.

## 6. Limitations

The blame scale is newly constructed and may simplify nuanced accounts of intergenerational policy, wealth, and institutions. Politically engaged respondents may be overrepresented, and common-method self-report can inflate associations. Doomscrolling may follow anger rather than precede it; ideology and family wealth may be measured imperfectly, leaving residual confounding. The design cannot determine causation. Future work should validate the blame measure across political groups and use repeated measures to test whether within-person changes in doomscrolling precede changes in attribution.

## 7. Alternative Narrative

Removing ideology, labelling neutral respondents as low blame, or highlighting only one provocative item could make doomscrolling seem to generate generational hostility. Such post hoc choices discard nuance and invite causal overstatement; preregistered composite scoring is preferable.
